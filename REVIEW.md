- [ ] **fa01** (both) Myth: Butter or cream soothes a burn. Fix: Cool it under running water for 20 minutes, as soon as you can. Source: [NHS: Burns and scalds](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)
- [ ] **fa02** (both) Myth: Popping a burn blister helps it heal. Fix: Do not burst burn blisters. Source: [NHS: Burns and scalds](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)
- [ ] **fa03** (both) Myth: A sticky bandage is best for a burn. Fix: Lay cling film over it. Do not wrap it around, and skip sticky dressings. Source: [NHS: Burns and scalds](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/)
- [ ] **fa04** (both) Myth: Tilt your head back to stop a nosebleed. Fix: Sit, lean forward, and pinch just above the nostrils for 10 to 15 minutes. Source: [NHS: Nosebleed](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/nosebleed/)
- [ ] **fa05** (both) Myth: Make someone throw up if they swallow poison. Fix: Get medical help right away. Do not try to make them sick. Source: [NHS: Poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa06** (both) Myth: Milk or water helps after poisoning. Fix: Do not give them anything to eat or drink. Source: [NHS: Poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa07** (both) Myth: Sucking out the venom helps. Fix: Never suck or cut the bite. Keep still and get help. Source: [NHS: Snake bites](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/snake-bites/)
- [ ] **fa08** (both) Myth: A tight band stops snake venom. Fix: Do not tie anything tightly around the bite. Loosen clothes instead. Source: [NHS: Snake bites](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/snake-bites/)
- [ ] **fa09** (both) Myth: Rub frostbite to warm it up. Fix: Use warm, not hot, water or body heat. Do not rub. Source: [NHS: Frostbite](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/frostbite/)
- [ ] **fa10** (both) Myth: Direct heat is best for frostbite. Fix: Do not use a fire, heater, hot water bottle, or hot bath. Source: [NHS: Frostbite](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/frostbite/)
- [ ] **fa11** (both) Myth: Heat helps a fresh sprain. Fix: Use ice and avoid heat for the first couple of days. Source: [NHS: Sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)
- [ ] **fa12** (both) Myth: Never let someone sleep after a head bump. Fix: For a minor head injury they can rest. An adult should stay with them for 24 hours. Source: [NHS: Head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/)
- [ ] **fa13** (both) Myth: Heat illness always passes with rest. Fix: If they are not better after 30 minutes of cooling, it may be heatstroke. Get help. Source: [NHS: Heat exhaustion and heatstroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/)
- [ ] **fa14** (both) Myth: Baking soda is a good sting remedy. Fix: Wash it, then use a cold pack. Skip home remedies like baking soda. Source: [NHS: Insect bites and stings](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/insect-bites-and-stings/)
- [ ] **fa15** (both) Myth: Pull out anything stuck in a wound. Fix: Leave it in. Press on both sides of it and get help. Source: [NHS: Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)
- [ ] **fa16** (both) Myth: Swap a soaked bandage for a fresh one. Fix: Add another cloth on top and keep the pressure on. Source: [NHS: Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)
- [ ] **fa17** (both) Myth: A hot bath fixes hypothermia. Fix: Wrap them in a dry blanket with the head covered. Warm drink if fully awake. Get help. Source: [NHS: Hypothermia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia/)
- [ ] **fa18** (both) Myth: Alcohol warms you up. Fix: Do not give alcohol. Give a warm non-alcohol drink if they are awake. Source: [NHS: Hypothermia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia/)
- [ ] **fa19** (both) Myth: Pee helps a jellyfish sting. Fix: Rinse with seawater, then soak in very warm water. Source: [NHS: Jellyfish and sea creature stings](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jellyfish-and-other-sea-creature-stings/)
- [ ] **fa20** (both) Myth: Ice is best for a jellyfish sting. Fix: Soak in very warm water, as hot as can be put up with, for 30 minutes. Source: [NHS: Jellyfish and sea creature stings](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/jellyfish-and-other-sea-creature-stings/)
- [ ] **fa21** (both) Myth: Put something in the mouth during a seizure. Fix: Never put anything in their mouth. Clear space and turn them on their side. Source: [CDC: First Aid for Seizures](https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html)
- [ ] **fa22** (both) Myth: Hold someone still during a seizure. Fix: Do not hold them down. Keep things away and time it. Source: [CDC: First Aid for Seizures](https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html)
- [ ] **fa23** (both) Myth: Seizures never need 911. Fix: Call 911 if it lasts more than 5 minutes, or it is their first seizure. Source: [CDC: First Aid for Seizures](https://www.cdc.gov/epilepsy/first-aid-for-seizures/index.html)
- [ ] **fa24** (both) Myth: Burn or smother a tick so it lets go. Fix: Grab it close to the skin with tweezers and pull with steady pressure. Source: [CDC: What to Do After a Tick Bite](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/after-a-tick-bite/index.html)
- [ ] **fa25** (both) Myth: Twist a tick to get it out. Fix: Pull steady and even. Do not twist or jerk. Source: [CDC: What to Do After a Tick Bite](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/after-a-tick-bite/index.html)
- [ ] **fa26** (both) Myth: If stroke signs go away, it is fine. Fix: Get medical help right away, even if signs stop. Source: [NHS: Stroke symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/stroke/symptoms/)
- [ ] **fa27** (both) Myth: A heart attack is always crushing chest pain. Fix: It can feel like indigestion, with feeling sick or short of breath. Source: [NHS: Heart attack symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heart-attack/symptoms/)
- [ ] **fa28** (both) Myth: An open garage door makes it safe. Fix: Never run a car in a garage attached to the house, even with the door open. Source: [CDC: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)
- [ ] **fa29** (both) Myth: You can smell carbon monoxide. Fix: It has no color or smell. Use a CO alarm. Source: [NHS: Carbon monoxide poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa30** (both) Myth: CPR does not work without rescue breaths. Fix: Call for help and give nonstop chest compressions. Source: [NHS: CPR](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/first-aid/cpr/)
- [ ] **fa31** (both) Myth: Gasping means they are breathing fine. Fix: Ignore odd gasps. If not breathing normally, call for help and start CPR. Source: [NHS: CPR](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/first-aid/cpr/)
- [ ] **fa32** (both) Myth: Stop CPR to fetch the AED yourself. Fix: Keep going and send someone else for it. Source: [NHS: CPR](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/first-aid/cpr/)
- [ ] **fa33** (both) Myth: Once you feel better you can get up. Fix: Stay lying down. Do not stand or walk, even if feeling better. Source: [NHS: Anaphylaxis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)
- [ ] **fa34** (both) Myth: Wait until it is bad to use an EpiPen. Fix: Use the auto-injector if you think it is anaphylaxis, even if mild. Source: [NHS: Anaphylaxis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)
- [ ] **fa35** (both) Myth: Stop all bleeding from a bite at once. Fix: Clean with soap and warm water. Gently squeeze so it bleeds a little. Source: [NHS: Animal and human bites](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/animal-and-human-bites/)
- [ ] **fa36** (both) Myth: Drowning is loud and easy to spot. Fix: Drowning happens in seconds and is often silent. Source: [CDC: Preventing Drowning](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)
- [ ] **fa37** (both) Myth: Eat before going in with a broken bone. Fix: Do not eat or drink in case you need surgery. Source: [NHS: Broken arm or wrist](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/broken-arm-or-wrist/)
- [ ] **fa50** (ya) Myth: A cold shower sobers someone up. Fix: Do not use a cold shower or bath. Get help and stay with them. Source: [NHS: Alcohol poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/alcohol-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa51** (ya) Myth: Coffee sobers you up. Fix: Do not give coffee or caffeine drinks. Source: [NHS: Alcohol poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/alcohol-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa52** (ya) Myth: Bread soaks up the alcohol. Fix: Do not give food. They could choke. Source: [NHS: Alcohol poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/alcohol-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa53** (ya) Myth: Making them throw up helps. Fix: Do not make them sick. Get help. Source: [NHS: Alcohol poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/alcohol-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa54** (ya) Myth: Feeling fine means you can play again. Fix: No contact sports for at least 3 weeks. Source: [NHS: Head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/)
- [ ] **fa55** (ya) Myth: A small head bump is fine for driving. Fix: Do not drive until you feel fully recovered. Source: [NHS: Head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/)
- [ ] **fa56** (ya) Myth: A stove in a tent is fine if it is cracked open. Fix: Never use a camp stove or grill inside a tent. Source: [NHS: Carbon monoxide poisoning](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carbon-monoxide-poisoning/)
- [ ] **fa57** (ya) Myth: Fast breathing first helps you hold your breath safely. Fix: Do not hyperventilate or hold your breath for long underwater. Source: [CDC: Preventing Drowning](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)
- [ ] **fa58** (ya) Myth: A few drinks on the water is no big deal. Fix: Avoid alcohol before or during swimming and boating. Source: [CDC: Preventing Drowning](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)
- [ ] **fa59** (ya) Myth: Pour juice into someone passed out from low sugar. Fix: If they are not awake, do not give food or drink. Use glucagon if you can, and call for help. Source: [NHS: Low blood sugar](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/low-blood-sugar-hypoglycaemia/)
- [ ] **fa60** (ya) Myth: Wrap a knocked-out tooth in a tissue. Fix: Hold it by the white part. Put it back in, or keep it in milk or spit. See a dentist fast. Source: [NHS: Knocked out tooth](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/knocked-out-tooth/)
- [ ] **fa61** (ya) Myth: No sweat means they are fine. Fix: Hot skin without sweat plus confusion can be heatstroke. Call for help and cool them. Source: [NHS: Heat exhaustion and heatstroke](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/heat-exhaustion-heatstroke/)
- [ ] **fa62** (ya) Myth: Heat and massage help a new sprain. Fix: Avoid heat, alcohol, and massage for the first couple of days. Source: [NHS: Sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)
- [ ] **fa63** (ya) Myth: Once it feels better, it is healed. Fix: Avoid hard exercise like running for up to 8 weeks. Source: [NHS: Sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/)
- [ ] **fa64** (ya) Myth: Driving them is faster and fine. Fix: Call for help. Keep them lying down; do not have them stand or walk. Source: [NHS: Anaphylaxis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/)
- [ ] **fa65** (ya) Myth: Lie down during an asthma attack. Fix: Sit up straight, stay calm, and use the reliever inhaler. Source: [NHS: Asthma attack](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/asthma/asthma-attack/)
- [ ] **fa66** (ya) Myth: If an asthma attack passes, no doctor needed. Fix: See a doctor within 2 days, even if you feel better. Source: [NHS: Asthma attack](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/asthma/asthma-attack/)
- [ ] **fa67** (ya) Myth: Ice is best for sunburn. Fix: Cool shower or damp towel, then aftersun. No ice, no petroleum jelly. Source: [NHS: Sunburn](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sunburn/)
- [ ] **fa68** (ya) Myth: Sit someone up right after they faint. Fix: If breathing normally, lay them on their back and raise their legs. Source: [NHS: Fainting](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fainting/)
- [ ] **fa69** (ya) Myth: Rub a chemical out of your eye. Fix: Run lots of water over the eye for at least 20 minutes. Do not rub. Source: [NHS: Eye injuries](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/eye-injuries/)

- [ ] **fa80** (adult) Myth: Sponge a feverish child with cold water. Fix: Do not undress or sponge them down. Fever is a normal response to infection. Source: [NHS: Fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/)
- [ ] **fa81** (adult) Myth: Sweat out a fever under blankets. Fix: Do not cover them in too many clothes or bedclothes. Source: [NHS: Fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/)

- [ ] **fa85** (adult) Myth: Drive a seizing child to the ER yourself. Fix: Do not drive them while they are still seizing. Call 911. Source: [NHS: Febrile seizures](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/febrile-seizures/)
- [ ] **fa86** (adult) Myth: Always reach in to pull out what they choke on. Fix: If they cough loudly, let them keep coughing and stay with them. Source: [NHS: How to stop a child from choking](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/first-aid-and-safety/first-aid/how-to-stop-a-child-from-choking/)

- [ ] **fa90** (adult) Myth: Steam helps croup. Fix: Do not put your child in a steamy room or have them breathe steam. Source: [NHS: Croup](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/croup/)

- [ ] **fa97** (adult) Myth: Rub cold hands and feet to warm them. Fix: Do not rub arms, legs, hands, or feet. Use a blanket and a warm drink. Source: [NHS: Hypothermia](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hypothermia/)
- [ ] **fa98** (adult) Myth: Floaties keep kids safe. Fix: Air or foam toys are not safety devices. Use life jackets and watch closely. Source: [CDC: Preventing Drowning](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)
- [ ] **fa99** (adult) Myth: Shallow bath water is safe for a minute. Fix: Watch closely and all the time, bathtubs too. Source: [CDC: Preventing Drowning](https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html)
- [ ] **fa100** (adult) Myth: A generator is fine in an open garage. Fix: Never use a generator in your home or garage, even with doors open. Source: [CDC: Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)
- [ ] **fa101** (adult) Myth: A small dose of paracetamol is fine for any baby. Fix: Do not give paracetamol to a child under 2 months. Source: [NHS: Fever in children](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-children/)

## Kitchen and Food Myths (78 cards)

- [ ] **kf01** (both) Myth: Rinsing raw chicken makes it cleaner. Fix: Raw chicken is ready to cook. Do not wash it. Source: [CDC: Chicken and Food Poisoning](https://www.cdc.gov/food-safety/foods/chicken.html)
- [ ] **kf02** (both) Myth: Water puts out a grease fire. Fix: Slide a lid over the pan and turn off the burner. Never use water. Source: [NFPA: Cooking safety](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/cooking)
- [ ] **kf03** (both) Myth: Lift the lid soon to check the fire. Fix: Do not remove the lid. Let the pan cool for a long time. Source: [NFPA: Cooking safety](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/cooking)
- [ ] **kf04** (both) Myth: Thawing on the counter is fine. Fix: Use the fridge, cold water, or the microwave. Never the counter. Source: [USDA FSIS: The Big Thaw](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/big-thaw-safe-defrosting-methods)
- [ ] **kf05** (both) Myth: Brown inside means a burger is done. Fix: Use a food thermometer. Ground beef needs 160 F. Source: [USDA FSIS: Kitchen Thermometers](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/kitchen-thermometers)
- [ ] **kf06** (both) Myth: Pink burger is always undercooked. Fix: At 160 F it is safe, even if pink. Source: [USDA FSIS: The Color of Meat and Poultry](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/color-meat-and-poultry)
- [ ] **kf07** (both) Myth: Pink poultry is never safe. Fix: All the meat is safe once every part reaches 165 F. Source: [USDA FSIS: The Color of Meat and Poultry](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/color-meat-and-poultry)
- [ ] **kf08** (both) Myth: Cool hot food on the counter first. Fix: Hot food can go right in the fridge, or an ice bath first. Source: [USDA FSIS: Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- [ ] **kf09** (both) Myth: Just put the whole pot in the fridge. Fix: Divide it into small, shallow containers so it cools fast. Source: [USDA FSIS: Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- [ ] **kf10** (both) Myth: Leftovers last a week. Fix: Eat them within 3 to 4 days, or freeze them. Source: [USDA FSIS: Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)
- [ ] **kf11** (both) Myth: Food is fine out if it looks OK. Fix: Never more than 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90 F. Source: [USDA FSIS: Danger Zone 40 F to 140 F](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/danger-zone-40f-140f)
- [ ] **kf12** (both) Myth: Cooked food left out overnight is fine. Fix: Throw it out. Source: [USDA FSIS: How Temperatures Affect Food](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/how-temperatures-affect-food)
- [ ] **kf13** (both) Myth: Cut the moldy spot off bread. Fix: Throw out moldy bread. Source: [USDA FSIS: Molds on Food](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/molds-food-are-they-dangerous)
- [ ] **kf14** (both) Myth: Any mold means toss hard cheese. Fix: For hard cheese, cut off at least 1 inch around and below the spot. Source: [USDA FSIS: Molds on Food](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/molds-food-are-they-dangerous)
- [ ] **kf15** (both) Myth: Sniff moldy food to check it. Fix: Do not sniff moldy food. Source: [USDA FSIS: Molds on Food](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/molds-food-are-they-dangerous)
- [ ] **kf16** (both) Myth: Past the date means unsafe. Fix: "Best if Used By" is about best flavor, not safety. Check for spoilage. Source: [USDA FSIS: Food Product Dating](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating)
- [ ] **kf17** (both) Myth: Sell-by means eat by. Fix: Sell-By is for the store. It is not a safety date. Source: [USDA FSIS: Food Product Dating](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating)
- [ ] **kf18** (both) Myth: A color change means meat is bad. Fix: Color change alone does not mean spoiled. Look for off smell, sticky or slimy feel. - [ ] **mn60** (ya) Myth: Starting age does not change much. Fix: Investor.gov shows about $127 a month from 18, but $441 from 35. Source: [Investor.gov: Introduction to Investing](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing)
- [ ] **mn61** (ya) Myth: Small savings do not add up. Fix: Investor.gov: one year of $1 a day ($365) grows to about $1,577 in 30 years at 5%. - [ ] **mn63** (ya) Myth: Missing one full payment costs nothing extra. Fix: You may lose the grace period that month and the month after. Source: [CFPB: What is a grace period for a credit card?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-grace-period-for-a-credit-card-en-47/)

- [ ] **mn87** (adult) Myth: Every mortgage has PMI. Fix: Usually with a conventional loan and less than 20% down. Source: [CFPB: What is private mortgage insurance?](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-private-mortgage-insurance-en-122/)

- [ ] **mn95** (adult) Myth: Insured bank money gets lost in failures. Fix: Since 1933, no depositor has lost a penny of FDIC-insured funds. Source: [FDIC: Understanding deposit insurance](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance/understanding-deposit-insurance)
- [ ] **mn96** (adult) Myth: Wait to hear from the bank. Fix: Contact your lender right away. Most will work with you. Source: [FTC: How to get out of debt](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-get-out-debt)

- [ ] **mn98** (adult) Myth: Creditors never settle for less. Fix: A creditor might negotiate and even accept less than you owe. Source: [FTC: How to get out of debt](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-get-out-debt)

- [ ] **sc97** (adult) Myth: Keep a grandkid's emergency secret. Fix: Scammers ask you to keep it secret. Contact family to check. Source: [FTC: Avoiding and Reporting Gift Card Scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/avoiding-and-reporting-gift-card-scams)
- [ ] **sc98** (adult) Myth: Mailed cash cannot be stopped. Fix: Contact the delivery service right away. Source: [FTC: What To Do if You Were Scammed](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed)
- [ ] **sc99** (adult) Myth: The IRS may DM you on social media. Fix: A social media direct message is never from the IRS. Source: [IRS: How to know it is the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/help/how-to-know-its-the-irs)
